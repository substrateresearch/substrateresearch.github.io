Research · The programme

Three questions, posed as questions.

The Institute organises its work around three aspects of intelligence — persistence, adaptation and transfer. They are posed below as questions, with the observations that would count as evidence for each. Nothing stronger is claimed, here or anywhere else on this site.

Aspect IPersist — remember what happened

The question: Can a system carry what happened forward, so that experience changes what it does next? Not knowledge placed in front of it — experience it accumulated itself, in the form of persistent state, recorded consequences and external memory.

What would count as evidence: later behaviour differs measurably when the recorded experience is present versus removed, under otherwise identical conditions.

Aspect IIAdapt — change without forgetting

The question: When the rules of its world change, does the system recover — and does it still remember how the world worked before? Adaptation and retention are tested together, because passing one by destroying the other is the commonest quiet failure in this field.

What would count as evidence: recovery under the new conditions while performance on the original conditions stays within a bound set before the test began.

Aspect IIITransfer — use it somewhere new

The question: Does what was learned here work over there — in situations the system was not trained or configured on, without further adjustment?

What would count as evidence: behaviour remains effective on genuinely unseen situations, scored against the same criteria as the familiar ones.

Together the three form a cycle. A system that cannot persist has nothing to adapt with. A system that cannot adapt without forgetting has nothing stable to transfer. A system that cannot transfer has learned a place, not a principle.

The detailed agendaFive lines of inquiry

The triad is the frame. These questions, inherited from the Institute's founding documents and kept because none has yet embarrassed itself, are the work underneath it.

Synthetic & adaptive systems

How do systems synthesise information across episodes or modalities and reorganise behaviour when objectives, tools or constraints change?

Architecture & substrate

What forms of state, memory and composition allow a system to learn from experience rather than merely repeat a fixed policy?

Agent engineering

What can be changed outside a model — tools, memory, feedback, environment and permissions — to produce more capable and more legible collective behaviour?

Model fidelity

Where do learned internal models of a task environment diverge from the real dynamics, and how does that divergence affect prediction and planning?

Experimental methodology

When do observations support an inference about capability, and when are they better explained by prompting, quirks of the environment, or evaluator choices?

Standing rule. Criteria before runs, results after runs, verdicts in the register either way. The Institute knows of no shorter description of the scientific method, and several longer ones it likes less.
