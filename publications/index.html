Technical report — 3N1 programme

The extra state earned nothing. 3N1 C003-L-ADAPT-001 · 16 November 2026.

What was tested. Whether a persistent environmental substrate — cumulative reward statistics and transition counts held in a database, updated atomically — improves adaptation to an unannounced reward reversal beyond simpler controllers, under a protocol frozen before execution: a hybrid controller (half cumulative mean, half most recent reward) against the cumulative controller it extends, with the programme’s original recency controller as the conventional baseline.

What was found. The first formal run failed at seed 119 — a file-descriptor leak in the substrate’s own database handling — and was preserved, diagnosed, corrected under a written amendment and re-run to completion. The hybrid then beat the cumulative controller on every seed, and tied the recency controller: identical post-change rewards on 99 of 100 seeds. The richer state added nothing the simplest mechanism did not already have.

What it does not establish. Anything about the substrate’s transition model, its variance estimates, or its other seven contexts — no controller used them. Their value is untested, not disproven, and no further substrate work proceeds until a task that needs them is specified.

A reader’s guide — ACCL

The compatibility layer. Agentic Acceleration Compatibility Layer · 16 November 2026.

What it describes. A runtime in which a program states what it needs — the result, the latency, the memory, the locality — and the machine introduces itself honestly: candidate plans are generated, the infeasible and the forbidden are removed, the survivor is chosen by a score that can be explained in a sentence, and every run is recorded as evidence.

What exists. A complete design on paper, argued with by its own companion document, and a working v0.1 proof of the whole path for one small model on one CPU — twelve tests, evidence saved, reproduced exactly on re-run. The code, this guide and the evidence are public.

What it does not establish. Performance, acceleration, or compatibility with real models. The proof proves the pipe, not the speed — and the guide says so in a section of its own, at the same size as the claim.

Technical report — Emerson programme

The worlds were not different enough. Emerson v0.14-transfer · 16 November 2026.

What was tested. Transfer: whether a GRU trained on one world (thrust gain 0.4, no drag), fine-tuned on a second (gain 0.8, drag ×0.90 per step) under the standard adaptation budget, beats the same architecture from scratch on an identical budget, against a bar fixed in the specification before any run.

What was found. The test never ran. Its environment check required the trained model to be bad on the new world before adaptation — zero-shot error above 0.340. It was 0.287: with no exposure to the second world at all, the model already outpredicted every baseline trained on it. The scratch control also barely learned at the comparison’s settings, so the bar it generated would have certified nothing. The kill rule closed the form before Stage 2.

What it does not establish. That transfer passed a bar — no bar was ever legitimately in front of it. The zero-shot figure is an observation from a voided test, and the report states it as one, at the same size as the death.

Technical report — 3N1 programme

Three hundred safe decisions. 3N1 C002 · 15 November 2026.

What was tested. Whether the full agent loop — role, visible environment, external memory — adapts to an unannounced reward reversal faster than the recency controller that benchmarks it, against a bar fixed in the protocol before any run.

What was found. The benchmark is measured: the recency controller stabilises in four runs out of ten, post-change mean reward 1.46. The first agent arm missed it on both measures, choosing the safe action on every trial of five runs, post-change mean reward 1.00. The second arm has not run.

What it does not establish. That external memory helps or does not help — no arm separates memory from prompted history — or anything beyond one task, one reversal, one model and five runs. The report says so itself, at the same size as the miss.

Technical report — Emerson programme

The advantage survives drift. Emerson v0.12-drift · 14 November 2026.

What was tested. Whether the gated recurrent model’s retention advantage — measured in v0.5 against a rule that changed once and then settled — survives a rule that drifts continuously for the whole episode, at three drift rates, against a bar fixed in the specification before any run.

What was found. The first attempt was void: the drift formula failed the specification’s own environment check, and the run was preserved as a void run rather than repaired into a result. On the corrected specification the GRU at hidden 256 cleared every part of the bar at the primary rate — retention 0.236 against a bar of 0.27, Phase 3 generalisation 0.375 against 0.439, adapted 0.259 against 0.313 — with every seed under every part of the bar, and passed on the mean at the two secondary rates, with one seed above its bar value at the fastest rate, stated in the report.

What it does not establish. Anything beyond a one-dimensional toy environment, three seeds, and the configuration tested. The report says so itself, at the same size as the pass.

Entry 001 — filed, not numbered

The Institute numbers only its own report series. This evaluation was published with Zenodo and keeps its publisher's record; it is filed here as the archive's founding entry under Independent evaluations.

FieldRecord
TitleIndependent Technical Evaluation of Externalized GPT-2 Parameters and Versioned Tensor Interventions
AuthorTony Vidlak
Published1 November 2026 · Version 1.0
SubjectWKClifford LLC — externalized GPT-2 parameter storage and versioned tensor interventions. The evaluated implementation remains the intellectual property of WKClifford LLC.
Permanent recordzenodo.org/records/23873689

What was tested. Whether GPT-2 parameters held outside the model can be materialised faithfully, whether inference on the relocated parameters reproduces the original, whether controlled tensor replacements create recoverable successor states, and how the system behaves when something is wrong.

What was found. All 148 GPT-2 tensors relocated byte-exactly; greedy inference agreed across 320 generated tokens, with a maximum observed absolute logit difference of approximately 2.01 × 10−4; parent and successor states persisted and were retrieved distinctly, with baseline parameters preserved. One limitation was documented, not excused: a successor view referencing a nonexistent replacement artifact failed later than it should have. After correction, the fidelity verification harness passed all specified acceptance conditions and issued a new verification receipt.

What it does not establish. Superior computational efficiency, energy savings, autonomous learning, or general architectural superiority — the report says so itself, in its own abstract, which the Institute considers the correct place for such sentences.

Publication categories

Technical reports describe implementation, experimental method, results and limitations. Independent evaluations document a tested artifact, environment, procedures and reproducibility evidence. Working papers present developing ideas and are identified, in letters of no unusual smallness, as preliminary.

How we identify a record

Each entry includes a title, named author(s), date, version, publication status and durable links. DOI citations will be included when assigned. Numbering is reserved for released reports; numbers are not assigned retroactively to unpublished work, however distinguished the work later becomes. Supporting source code and reproducibility materials will be linked directly from individual publication records when available.

Meanwhile, the laboratory's running record — programmes, verdicts and deaths — is kept current in Projects and the Obituaries.
