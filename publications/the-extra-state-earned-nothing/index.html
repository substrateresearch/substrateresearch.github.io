Publications · Technical report — 3N1 programme

The extra state earned nothing.

3N1 C003-L-ADAPT-001 · 16 November 2026. A benchmark of three numerical controllers ran twice: once into a failure of the Institute’s own substrate at seed 119, and once to completion. This report leads with the failure, because the failure is the better evidence.

The 3N1 programme has spent considerable effort proving that an environmental substrate can retain information reliably. This experiment asked the harder question: whether the retained information produces behaviour that a simpler mechanism cannot. Three controllers faced the programme’s standard task — an unannounced reward reversal halfway through sixty trials — under a protocol frozen before execution. One of them carried the substrate’s full numerical state. It tied the controller that carries almost none.

The run that failed at seed 119

The first formal execution did not finish. It stopped during the cumulative controller’s seed 119, on a database error: the machine reported it could not open the run’s database file. By then the recency controller had completed all 100 of its runs and the cumulative controller 18. The third controller had not started. No aggregate was written.

The interrupted evidence was preserved exactly as it lay, and inspected before anything was repaired. Disk space and file limits were not exhausted. The interrupted run’s own database was internally consistent at 48 of its 60 observations — every count agreed with every other. The defect was then reproduced under controlled conditions: across 600 operations, the process’s open file descriptors climbed from 4 to 605, and returned to 4 only when the garbage collector ran. The cause sits in a standard idiom: in Python, a database connection used as a context manager commits or rolls back its transaction but does not close the connection. The substrate’s inspection routine opened a connection on every call and closed none of them, so descriptors accumulated across the single benchmark process until the operating system’s limit — 1,024 — refused the next one, mid-run, at seed 119.

The correction followed the programme’s amendment discipline. The original implementation was left untouched and its identity verified by hash. The fix was written as a versioned successor, documented in a formal amendment (Amendment 001) that records the failure, the diagnosis and the correction, and states what did not change: no reward mapping, no decision rule, no exploration probability, no seed, no acceptance criterion. The corrected code held its descriptor count at 4 across the same 600 operations, and passed consistency, rollback, reopening and integrity validation. The benchmark then ran again from the beginning, into a new evidence directory; the interrupted directory remains where it fell.

The corrected execution completed all 300 runs. An independent evaluation afterwards recomputed every figure from the trial-level records rather than trusting the summaries: 18,000 trial records, 900 receipt hashes verified, 200 run databases integrity-checked, zero failures. The same investigation that produced this report’s negative finding also demonstrated that its evidence could survive a failed experiment and a corrected rerun. The Institute regards the second demonstration as the more valuable of the two.

The experiment

The substrate under test is a persistent numerical store: for each of eight contexts and three actions it keeps a running mean reward, a reward variance, an observation count and the most recent change, all in double-precision floating point, alongside counts of transitions between contexts — the raw material of an environmental model, held in a database with atomic updates. The task used one context. Three controllers decided:

Recency — the programme’s original controller, imported unchanged from the E001 harness: act on the most recently observed reward for each action. It keeps one number per action. Cumulative — act on the substrate’s cumulative mean reward per action: the whole history, averaged. Hybrid — act on the average of the two: half cumulative mean, half most recent reward.

Exploration was identical for all three — each untried action once, then a fixed 12% random choice — and the seeds were matched, 101 to 200, one hundred runs per controller. The frozen primary criterion: the hybrid must beat the cumulative controller on mean paired post-change reward, with the 95% bootstrap confidence interval excluding zero. The protocol required, separately, a demonstrated advantage over recency before any claim of superiority to the conventional controller.

The primary criterion was met, and it is the least interesting sentence in this report. Hybrid minus cumulative: +2.544 mean paired difference, confidence interval 2.370 to 2.726, better on all 100 seeds. But the cumulative controller was a weak comparator for an abrupt reversal: an average over the whole history can adapt eventually, yet thirty trials of the old mapping outweigh the new one for most of the second half. It chose the pre-change winner 1,381 times after that action had turned against it, and never stabilised in 86 runs of 100. Beating it demonstrates that recent outcomes matter. It does not demonstrate that the substrate matters.

The consequential comparison is hybrid against recency. Mean paired difference: −0.002, interval −0.007 to 0.000. The paired post-change rewards are identical on 99 of 100 seeds. The entire aggregate difference is the size of a single action: across 3,000 post-change decisions the two controllers’ action counts differ by exactly one choice — one occasion on which the hybrid took the old winner where recency took the new one, a seven-point swing on one trial of one run. Identical rewards do not by themselves prove identical trajectories, and a paired trial-level comparison is retained as a planned verification; the counts already show the trajectories differ at least once, and the conclusion does not depend on where.

Stated without qualification: on this task, the extra environmental state did not earn its complexity. A persistent, atomic, double-precision substrate — and a controller that remembers one number per action — produced the same behaviour, because everything the winning controllers used was the most recent outcome, and everything else the substrate kept went unused.

What was not tested

No controller’s decisions used the transition model. None used the reward-variance estimates. Seven of the substrate’s eight contexts were never entered. Those components are therefore untested, not falsified — and the distinction sets the programme’s next gate. No further substrate implementation will be treated as research progress until a task is specified in which multiple contexts have a genuine causal role, the necessary features are named, the simpler controller expected to fail is identified, and a conventional model-based method receiving the same information is included as a control. If that specification cannot be written, this line of development stops here, with this report as its result.
