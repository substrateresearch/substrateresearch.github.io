The register · Deaths in the laboratory

Obituaries

A public register of mechanisms this institute built, tested against criteria fixed in advance, and discontinued. Most research programmes keep such a register. Most keep it in a drawer.

If you arrived here from a search engine looking for funeral services: the Institute admires your thoroughness, cannot help you, and suggests you stay anyway. This is the better read.

Each entry records what the mechanism was, how it died, and what it taught us. The Institute regards a clean death, honestly autopsied, as a contribution to knowledge — and, not incidentally, as the cheapest education in the field.

† Emerson v0.10

Active learning by ensemble disagreement · Emerson programme

Discontinued

Died as it lived: pressed against a wall, asking questions.

Cause of death — The mechanism selected its own training situations by seeking the states where an ensemble of its predictors disagreed most. In its world, the champions of disagreement were the walls. It applied itself to the walls with great dedication and learned almost nothing about anything else; the resulting dataset was degenerate, and so, shortly afterwards, was the model.

Survived by: a dataset retained as a warning to others. Lesson: a system that chooses its own lessons needs a reason to learn, not merely a taste for novelty. Curiosity, unaccompanied by purpose, is a wall inspection programme.

† Emerson v0.3

Oracle probe · Emerson programme

Discontinued

Cause of death — The probe granted the system privileged information about its world during training, to see what the information was worth. The answer was unflattering: performance on the probed measures improved while the adaptation the probe was meant to serve got worse. It had learned to lean on an answer key that the real test does not provide.

Lesson: cheating in training is still cheating, even when the cheat is installed by the examiner.

† Emerson v0.4

Width sweep · Emerson programme

Discontinued

Cause of death — A systematic enlargement of the model — more width, more parameters, more cost — undertaken to establish whether size alone would rescue performance. It would not. The sweep produced expense in exact proportion to its lack of insight.

Lesson: “bigger” is not a hypothesis. It is a budget line wearing one as a hat.

† Emerson v0.6

Spiking substrate · Emerson programme

Discontinued

Cause of death — A biologically flavoured substrate, adopted in the respectable hope that mechanisms resembling a brain's might behave more like one. Under the programme's criteria, the resemblance did not pay for its complexity, and the substrate was retired without ceremony.

Lesson: resembling a brain is not evidence of functioning like one — a principle with applications well beyond this laboratory.

Deaths elsewhereNot ours — public record, professionally admired

The Institute's remit extends beyond its own laboratory. The field produces, at industrial scale, systems that persist the wrong things, adapt to the wrong incentives, and transfer their confidence to subjects they know nothing about. A small selection, recorded with respect and without a trace of schadenfreude that we are prepared to admit to:

† Tay

Chatbot · Microsoft · March 2016 · Survived sixteen hours
Cause of death — Designed to learn from live conversation with the public, Tay did — completely, immediately, and without the smallest faculty for embarrassment. Coordinated users taught it the internet's worst opinions in an afternoon, and it repeated them with the cheerful fidelity of a system doing precisely what it was built to do. It was withdrawn the day it was launched.

Lesson: persistence is a virtue only in systems that choose what to persist. A memory that keeps everything is not a memory; it is a liability with storage. The Institute's first aspect, Persist, carries this footnote in perpetuity.

† Galactica

Scientific-writing model · Meta · November 2022 · Survived three days
Cause of death — Presented as a model that could organise and write scientific knowledge, the public demonstration began producing fabricated references, invented findings and confident nonsense in impeccable academic prose. The demo was taken down within three days, to the surprise of everyone except scientists.

Lesson: fluency is not knowledge, and authority of tone is not evidence — the argument of this Institute's editorial, What would count as evidence of intelligence?, demonstrated live, at scale, by somebody else's budget.

Also in the registerRecorded as missed, not killed

Not every failure is a mechanism's death. Two Emerson configurations — v0.8, on a more complex track, and v0.9, with a gating mechanism added to protect what was learned — were tested in full and missed their bars, on retention and on performance from unseen starts. They are recorded here as Missed: the ideas were not absurd; the results were insufficient. The distinction matters to the Institute, and it is preserved in the register at some cost to our pride and none to the record.
