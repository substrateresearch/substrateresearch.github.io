Evaluations · Evidence & reproducibility

Independent evaluation, including of ourselves.

An evaluation is useful when another person can understand what was tested, how it was tested, what passed, and what remains unresolved. The Institute applies this standard to other people's systems on request, and to its own systems without being asked — which is how the Obituaries got so well populated.

The standard

We distinguish artifact inspection from execution testing, and a passed test from a broader claim about a system. Reports identify the tested version, the relevant environment, the input cases, the procedures, the measurements and the known limitations. Conclusions are stated as narrowly as the evidence permits — “the controller recovered after the outage in this benchmark” is a finding; “the controller understands failure” is a horoscope.

The verdict format

Every evaluation concludes with the same three verdicts, per aspect, so results can be compared across systems and across time: Passed, Missed, and Not verified. As a specimen, here is the Institute's current verdict on the one configuration of its own Emerson programme that has cleared a full bar — in a simple environment, a qualification we print in the same size type as the verdict:

Completed evaluations

One independent evaluation is already complete, and it is on the public record rather than in a drawer: Independent Technical Evaluation of Externalized GPT-2 Parameters and Versioned Tensor Interventions (Tony Vidlak, 1 November 2026, deposited with Zenodo). The subject was WKClifford LLC's externalized parameter storage; the findings — byte-exact tensor relocation, reproducible inference under the tested conditions, recoverable versioned interventions, and one documented validation limitation, re-tested after correction — are stated in the report as narrowly as the evidence permits, including the claims the evidence does not support. It is filed as the founding entry of the publication archive, and it is, for the moment, the Institute's entire track record in evaluating other people's systems: one engagement, one report, zero findings softened.

Reproducibility

Where rights and confidentiality permit, reports provide checksums, scripts, configurations and links to preserved artifacts. When materials cannot be released, the resulting limitations are explained rather than omitted. Results the Institute reports about its own programmes are independently recalculated from the underlying records before publication — see 3N1.

Commissioned evaluations

The Institute also evaluates systems built by others, under the same framework: a fixed scope and protocol agreed in writing before testing, and a written report stating what passed, what missed, and what could not be verified. There is no badge, no seal, and no guarantee; clients receive findings as found, and their material remains theirs. Independence is easier to keep when it is structural: the research published on this site carries no sales copy, and no evaluation verdict is available for purchase at any price.

Commissioned evaluations are described in a one-page offer sheet available on request through the Institute's existing channels. Public evaluations appear in the publication archive only after authorship and release status are confirmed.
