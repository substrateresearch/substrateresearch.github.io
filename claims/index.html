The register · Promises, filed and graded

The Claims Ledger

A standing public register of dated, falsifiable claims about machine intelligence — filed verbatim, with the date they were made and the date they come due, and graded when that date arrives: Kept, Missed, or Quietly Forgotten. No commentary beyond the grade. The record is the satire.

The Institute files its own claims here too, under the same rules, because a ledger that grades everyone except its keeper is a hit list, not a record. A claim that was never dated is not filed — it cannot be graded, which is usually why it was never dated.

⚑ Rules of the ledger

• Filed verbatim, with source. Paraphrase is not a claim.
• A due date is part of the claim. “Soon”, “eventually”, and “in the visible future” are not due dates and are not filed.
• Graded on the claim as made, on the date it came due — not on what happened decades later, and not on what the claimant meant to say.
• Kept — the thing happened, on time, as stated. Missed — the date passed and it had not. Quietly Forgotten — the date passed, it had not happened, and the claim was never retracted, only stopped being mentioned.

Entry 001 — The world’s chess champion, within ten years

Simon & Newell · Made 1957 · Due 1967

Missed

The claim, verbatim: “That within ten years a digital computer will be the world’s chess champion, unless the rules bar it from competition.” — Herbert A. Simon, with Allen Newell, in a 1957 speech published as “Heuristic Problem Solving: The Next Advance in Operations Research”, Operations Research, Jan–Feb 1958.

Grade — Missed — No computer was world chess champion in 1967. One eventually was — Deep Blue beat Garry Kasparov in 1997, forty years after the claim, by brute-force search and opening databases, not by the general problem-solving methods Simon and Newell were pursuing. The ledger grades the claim as made, on the date it came due. The eventual achievement is recorded here, and does not change the grade.

Sources: Quote Investigator, “Prediction About AI Systems”; Simon & Newell, Operations Research 6(1), 1958.

Entry 002 — Watson, an AI doctor, within 18 to 24 months

IBM · Made 2011 · Due 2012–2013

Missed

The claim, as reported: Watson’s first commercial offerings for health care would be available in 18 to 24 months — IBM’s phrasing, as reported by IEEE Spectrum, the day after Watson beat two human champions on Jeopardy! and IBM announced a new career for its quiz-show winner in medicine.

Grade — Missed — The projects announced that first day did not yield commercial products. The oncology work that followed ended in acrimony: MD Anderson Cancer Center cancelled its Watson project in 2016 after spending $62 million (2016 University of Texas audit). A 2018 Wall Street Journal investigation found no published research showing Watson improving patient outcomes, and more than a dozen partners had halted or shrunk their oncology projects.

Sources: IEEE Spectrum, “How IBM Watson Overpromised and Underdelivered on AI Health Care”; Wall Street Journal, August 2018.

Entry 003 — Over a million robotaxis on the road, next year for sure

Tesla · Made 22 April 2019 · Due end of 2020

Quietly Forgotten

The claim, verbatim: “If you fast forward a year, maybe a year and three months, but next year for sure, we’ll have over a million robotaxis on the road.” — Elon Musk, Tesla Autonomy Day, 22 April 2019.

Grade — Quietly Forgotten — The end of 2020 arrived. The Tesla Network did not launch. No robotaxi fleet of any size entered commercial service. Tesla did not formally retract the target; it simply ceased to be referenced. (For the record: as of 2026, Tesla’s Austin robotaxi testing numbers in the hundreds, not the million.)

Sources: The Drive, “Tesla Network Promises One Million Robotaxis on the Road by 2020”, 23 Apr 2019; Fast Company, “Elon Musk’s failed Tesla robotaxi promise is the height of self-driving hype”, 18 Sep 2021.

Entry 004 — An LLM arm will beat the recency controller, on both columns

Substrate Research · Made 10 November 2026 · Due on completion of each arm

The claim, as filed in advance: an LLM agent arm of the Institute’s C002 benchmark will beat the deterministic recency controller on both pre-set metrics — median time-to-stable-optimal below 41.5, and post-change mean reward above 1.457. The bar was fixed in the C002 protocol on 2026-11-10, before any LLM arm had run.

Grade, Axiom — Missed — Five seeds, five identical scores: total 60, post-change mean 1.00, never stabilised on any seed. Published on the homepage 2026-11-11, commit 438ea85.

Grade, Vela — Open — Delayed; its evidence database declined to open on its first seed, for reasons not yet established. Rerun pending. The grade will be entered when it runs.

Source: the Institute’s own record — C002 protocol and results, kept on this site.

The tally, for the record. The ledger opens with no Kept entries. That is not an oversight; a Kept will be earned, not asserted. The ledger grows one verified entry at a time.

Deliberately not filedResults are not promises

AlphaFold 2 (CASP14, 2020, median GDT 92.4; 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry) is not filed here. It is a result, not a dated promise filed in advance, and the ledger grades promises, not achievements after the fact. If a dated claim about AlphaFold’s successors is filed, it will be graded like any other.
