From the Institute · A journal of record

We build small intelligences, and publish exactly how they disappoint us.

Substrate Research is an independent institute studying whether machines can be said to persist, adapt, and transfer — and what would count as evidence either way. We fix the pass mark before each experiment, and we publish the result after it, whichever way it falls. The wider industry regards this as an eccentric practice. We regard it as record-keeping.

And yes — since you are wondering, because everyone wonders — this is a real research institute, those are real experiments, and the obituaries are real too. We checked. Twice. The second time was for us.

The frameworkThree questions, asked of everything we build

Every system in this institute is examined on the same three aspects of intelligence. They form a cycle: what a system cannot keep, it cannot adapt; what it cannot adapt, it cannot use anywhere new.

Aspect I

Persist

Remember what happened. Knowledge in a prompt is not memory. Evidence: later behaviour differs measurably when the recorded experience is present versus removed, under otherwise identical conditions.

Aspect II

Adapt

Change without forgetting. Anyone can improve at the new thing by abandoning the old. Evidence: recovery under changed conditions while original performance stays within a bound set before the test.

Aspect III

Transfer

Use it somewhere new. A memorised solution is not a capability. Evidence: behaviour remains effective in situations the system was not trained or configured on, without further adjustment.

⚑ Open for predictions — the next experiment

Does recorded experience actually help, or does a simple note of the most recent events do just as well? Our programme 3N1 has prepared a controlled benchmark comparing adaptation from recorded experience against a simpler recency-based controller. The pass criteria were fixed before the run. The run has not happened yet.

Readers are invited to register a prediction — pass, miss, or death by wall — and keep their own score at home. We shall keep ours here, in public, either way. Predicting the outcome of an experiment before it runs is, in the trade, called preregistration. We find it concentrates the mind wonderfully.

From the obituariesLatest death in the laboratory

† Emerson v0.10

Active learning by ensemble disagreement · Discontinued after testing

Died as it lived: pressed against a wall, asking questions.

Cause of death — The mechanism chose its own training situations by seeking maximum disagreement among its predictors. The most disagreed-about places in its world were the walls. It therefore studied the walls, exclusively, at length, while its education in everything else quietly starved.

The full register—four formal deaths, two recorded misses, and the lessons each left behind—is kept in the Obituaries. We recommend it to anyone who has been told that a research programme is a sequence of successes.

Meanwhile, in the wider fieldOur remit is not limited to our own laboratory

The Institute's own failures are documented above out of duty. The field's failures are documented below out of something closer to affection. Two favourites from the public record, both lovingly autopsied in full in the Obituaries:

March 2016 · Microsoft

Tay, chatbot, aged sixteen hours

Released to learn from conversation with the public. Learned from conversation with the public. Withdrawn the same day, having acquired, at production speed, every opinion the internet keeps in its basement. Persist was achieved; nobody had specified what was worth persisting.

November 2022 · Meta

Galactica, science writer, aged three days

A model presented as an organiser of scientific knowledge, withdrawn within days after confidently fabricating it. Fluent, authoritative, and wrong in complete sentences — a combination the Institute's third essay had, coincidentally, already titled: Intelligence Is Not What a System Knows.

A note on tone, for new readers. The jokes on this site are ours, and most of them are at our expense. The data is real, the criteria are fixed in advance, and nothing here is published that the underlying records do not support. Satire is the house style. Fabrication would be the house scandal.
