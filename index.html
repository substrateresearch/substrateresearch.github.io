Investigating the substrates of intelligence.

Substrate Research is an independent initiative examining how computational systems represent, adapt, remember and act—and how those capabilities can be evaluated with evidence rather than assumption.

Our premise

Intelligence is a question to investigate, not a label to assume.

Modern machine learning has demonstrated remarkable capabilities, but important questions remain about architecture, state, agency, environmental interaction and reliable evaluation. We investigate those questions through independent experiments and technical analysis.

Our aim is not to declare that a particular architecture produces intelligence. It is to develop testable systems, document their behavior and make credible findings accessible.

Three aspects of intelligence

Persist. Adapt. Transfer.

We pose intelligence as three testable aspects, arranged in a cycle. A system that displays none of them may be capable, but its behavior gives us little reason to call it intelligent—and each aspect can be tested, and can fail, on evidence.

01 / PERSIST

Remember what happened

Can a system carry experience forward, so what happened survives a single interaction? We investigate persistent state, recorded consequences and external memory—and how later behavior changes when they are present, and when they are removed.

02 / ADAPT

Change without forgetting

When conditions or rules change, can a system update its behavior while retaining what remains true? We test adaptation and retention together: improving under new conditions at the cost of earlier competence is not adaptation, it is replacement.

03 / TRANSFER

Use it somewhere new

Does what was learned hold beyond the situation in which it was learned—new starting points, changed conditions, different decisions? Behavior that only works where it was acquired is a local result, not a general one.

What a system does in a new situation becomes new experience: to persist, to adapt from, and to test again. Our projects, evaluations and publications are organized around these three aspects. See the research agenda →
