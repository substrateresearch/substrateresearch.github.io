About · The initiative

A place for careful, independent inquiry.

Substrate Research is a publication and experimental research initiative focused on computational intelligence and the systems that enable adaptive behaviour. It is independent, and does not claim affiliation with a university or established research institution — a fact stated proudly, since independence is the whole arrangement.

Why this exists

Questions about intelligence deserve experiments that can fail, mechanisms that can be inspected, and results that can be revisited. This initiative brings together architecture exploration, engineering prototypes and evaluation reports under a consistent public record — and writes the record in a voice intended to be read by humans, including the parts where the humans were wrong.

Editorial principles

Attribution. Every research publication must identify its actual contributors and authors.

Transparency. Preliminary theories, reproducible findings and external evaluations are clearly distinguished.

Bounded claims. Conclusions must not exceed the evidence supplied. This principle is enforced most strictly against the Institute itself, which is why its homepage reports a score of one.

Confidentiality. Unreleased research, third-party materials and private data remain outside this public website.

Tone. The Institute permits itself jokes. It does not permit itself inventions. The narrator further reserves the right to address the reader directly, to mock the Institute at least as often as anyone else, and to break the fourth wall whenever the wall is load-bearing. The narrator does not reserve the right to invent results, round a failure up into a success, or describe a wall as a learning opportunity. Where the two might be confused, the record — criteria, verdicts, and the register of the dead — is available for inspection.
