Research program · Adaptive systems

Emerson

A small, trainable world model that learns how an environment responds to its actions—built to test whether adaptation can grow from interaction, rather than be assumed from scale.

Emerson is not a language model and not a pretrained system. It begins knowing nothing about its environment. Everything it comes to predict is learned from the consequences of its own actions, inside that environment.

The question

Can a system that starts from nothing learn how its world behaves, act on what it has learned, and then adapt when the rules change—without losing what was true before? And does what it learned in one situation still hold in situations it has never encountered?

How it is tested

Each version of the system is judged in three stages, against criteria set before the run. First, prediction: can it forecast the effects of actions in situations held back from its training? Second, adaptation and retention: when a rule of the environment is reversed, does its behavior recover—and does it still perform under the original rule afterwards? Third, transfer: does it remain effective from starting points it has never seen?

Approaches that miss their criteria are discontinued and the outcome is recorded as a result in its own right. A failed mechanism, clearly bounded, is evidence—not an embarrassment to be edited out.

Where it sits in the triad

Persist. The system carries a state that is updated by every interaction; nothing is learned from a single isolated example. Adapt. Adaptation with retention is Emerson's central test: changing is easy, changing without forgetting is the claim under examination. Transfer. Performance from unseen starting points decides whether what was learned is a model of the environment or a memory of particular episodes.

Status

Exploratory. One model configuration has met its full set of criteria in a simple environment; in more complex environments results are mixed, and several candidate mechanisms have been tested and discontinued under the criteria above. The next step is a harder environment, not a larger version of a result that already passed.

Release status: This page describes the program's question, test design and bounded status. Detailed results, parameters and methods will be published only after review for release, in the publication archive. Nothing here is a claim of general intelligence.
