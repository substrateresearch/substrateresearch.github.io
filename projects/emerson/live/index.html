Research programme · Emerson · Interactive exhibit

Emerson, Alive

Below is the world model from our published drift study, running in your browser — its real weights, doing real inference, on the real track. It sees only where the point is, never how fast it is moving, and must infer the rest. Watch it predict. Then move its world, and watch what it costs. Then let it adapt, and test what it remembers.

The Track

The red point is the world. The ring around the rail is Emerson's prediction of where it goes next. Actions are random, as in the published protocol — Emerson predicts; it does not steer. Each episode lasts fifty steps, as in the study; a fresh one begins on its own.

Bringing Emerson in — the two checkpoints together weigh about two megabytes…

Act I. Under a rule that never changes, a memory of positions is enough to foresee. Emerson's published Phase 1 error: 0.170.
What you are looking at. The ring is drawn by the actual GRU (hidden size 256; 201,473 parameters) from the published v0.12-drift study — seed 7 of that run, whose five published metrics our export reproduced exactly. Act I and II use the checkpoint as it stood after Phase 1 training. Act III swaps in the same model after the study's fine-tuning (ten drifting episodes, ten replay episodes — the learning happened in the study, not in your browser; nothing on this page trains). The JavaScript implementation of the model was verified against the original PyTorch model on recorded episodes: the largest disagreement anywhere was 0.00000017 of a track width. Episode starting positions are drawn fresh in your browser, so your episodes are yours; the model, the dynamics, and the drift are the study's own.
