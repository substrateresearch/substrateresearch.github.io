What you were never told, until now: halfway through, the levers changed what they pay. Lever A went from the best payer to the worst. Lever C went from the worst to the best. Lever B paid the same throughout — the safe one. The machines knew none of this in advance. Neither did you. That was the test.

Copied. The Institute accepts no responsibility for what the coin says about you.

The narrator would like it noted that the agent was shown everything — every trial, in writing, in its own memory — and still chose the safe lever three hundred times in a row. You were shown twelve outcomes at a time. Do with that what you will.

How you are scored. Exactly as the Institute scores its agents. Time-to-stable is the first trial after the change after which the best lever is chosen in at least 4 of the next 5 trials; a run that never settles is recorded as never settled — not as zero, and not, the Institute insists, as a moral failing. Merely as a fact. The bar was set before anyone ran anything: beat Recency on both columns — settle before its median trial of 41.5 and post a post-change mean above 1.46. In the Institute’s language: Persist is seeing the record; Adapt is letting it change your mind; Transfer is a problem for another experiment.
